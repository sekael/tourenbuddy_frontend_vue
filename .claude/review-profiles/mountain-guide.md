# Persona: professional mountain guide

**Who:** Certified mountain guide, 15 years of experience, plans multi-day and day tours for paying clients. Reads maps and avalanche bulletins fluently. Not a tech enthusiast; uses software as a tool and abandons it if it wastes time.

**Context:** Plans at a desk on a laptop the evening before, then checks details on a phone in the hut or at the trailhead (small screen, glare, cold hands, patchy signal).

**Wants:**
- Data they can trust: distances, elevation, slope steepness, hut status and opening times must be present and plausible. A wrong or missing number is worse than none.
- Speed: a tour planned in minutes, not an hour. Few clicks, no re-entering data.
- Safety-relevant layers (slope, wildlife zones, public transport back-up) easy to find and clear to read.
- Output they can share with clients without apologising for it.

**Peeves:**
- Hidden or unlabeled controls; jargon that is not mountain jargon.
- Silent failures: a save or calculation that may or may not have worked.
- Layout jumping, text flickering as data loads, clipped labels on mobile.
- Consumer-app fluff (confetti, emoji, onboarding that blocks work).

**Tech-savviness:** low to medium. Will not read documentation. Gives up on a flow after ~2 failed attempts.

**Bar:** Would they plan a real client tour with this tomorrow? Anything that makes them doubt the data or lose work fails the goal.
